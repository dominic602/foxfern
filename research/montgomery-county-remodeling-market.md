# Can Montgomery County Support a $10M/Year Remodeler?

Prepared 2026-10-27 for Fox & Fern Property Services. Research via Exa Agent; sources linked inline.

## Short answer

**Yes. The market is big enough: $10M is about 1% of what Montgomery County homeowners spend on professional home improvements each year.** The proof is that at least one firm in the county already does more than twice that ($26.5M). The harder part is the business model, not demand: **$10M needs a blended average of roughly $35,000–$40,000 per major job, about 235–270 major projects a year**, and the sales, project-management and crew capacity to deliver them.

| Delaware | 151,016 | $89,546 | $332,200 | 1957 | 79% |

Montgomery County is close to an ideal remodeling market:
- **Wealthy:** median household income about 45% above the U.S. median.
- **High home values:** owners have equity to borrow against.
- **Old housing:** median build year 1971, so most homes are due for kitchens, baths, systems and layout updates.
- **Little new construction:** only 1,600 new units were built in 2025 ([county data](https://assets.montgomerycountypa.gov/files/2026-10/2025%20Housing%20Units%20Built_AOD.pdf)), so demand goes into improving existing homes.

## 3. Market size

**Total professional improvement spending** = owner households × spending per owner × 84.1% professional share

| Area | At $4,700/owner (national avg) | At $6,000/owner (adjusted for MontCo incomes) |
|---|---|---|
| **Montgomery County** | **$947M / year** | **$1.21B / year** |
| Your realistic service area (MontCo + Chester + Berks + Philadelphia) | $3.4B / year | $4.3B / year |

**The part a full-service interior remodeler can reach.** Total spending includes roofs, HVAC, windows and similar work that specialists capture. Assuming 35–50% is kitchens, baths, basements, whole-home, additions and interior work (my assumption, not a published figure):

| Montgomery County addressable market | **$330M – $600M / year** |
|---|---|
| **$10M as a share of it** | **1.7% – 3.0%** |
| $10M as a share of *all* professional improvement spending in MontCo | **~1.1%** |

Fox & Fern also does exteriors, repairs and commercial work, which pushes the real addressable number higher.

Fox & Fern's current offer, with bathrooms from $5,000, is in between, so the mix matters.

The average NAHB remodeler does **$2.7M**. At $10M you'd be well above average, roughly 3.7× the typical firm. The Top 500 full-service median is 22 employees ([source](https://www.qualifiedremodeler.com/2025-top-500-full-service-fits-and-starts/)).

## 6. Risks and headwinds

- **National growth is flat.** JCHS calls remodeling spending "stagnating", at +1.5–2.4% a year. Growth has to come from taking share, not from a rising market.
- **Low job values cap growth.** At a $15,000 average you'd need 667 jobs a year, the volume model, which requires a very different operation.
- **The Main Line high end is crowded** (Harth, Gardner/Fox, Hynes, Fedor). The 422 corridor has less competition (see `keywords/home-remodeling-montgomery-county.md`) but lower incomes than Lower Merion or Gwynedd.
- **Capacity is the real bottleneck.** About 5 major jobs a week needs roughly 4–6 project managers, dedicated sales/estimating staff, and reliable trade partners. These numbers are planning estimates, not published benchmarks.

## 7. Gaps in this data

- Montgomery County's firm count and receipts for residential remodelers (NAICS 236118) couldn't be retrieved. The Census API is blocked from this environment.
- Harvard JCHS's 2023 Philadelphia-metro spending figure exists in their interactive tool, but the exact value wasn't retrievable. Philadelphia historically averaged about $3,200 per owner (1994–2015, inflation-adjusted), similar to the national average at the time.
- No countywide count of alteration permits exists in a single published source.
- Close rate, lead-to-estimate ratio and revenue per employee are planning assumptions, not verified benchmarks.
